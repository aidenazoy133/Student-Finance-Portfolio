# Week of February 9-15, 2026

This was my first full week actually tracking markets daily and honestly it's way more confusing than I expected. Not in a bad way, just in a "wow there's a lot happening all at once" way.

The biggest thing I'm trying to wrap my head around is this rotation concept. Apple dropped almost 6 bucks on Tuesday, Microsoft got downgraded hard, and everyone kept saying money was moving out of tech and into healthcare or other sectors. But like, why? And is it something people plan or is it just what we call it after the fact when we look back at the data? I'm not totally sure I get the mechanics of it yet. Does money literally flow from one sector to another or do different investors just get interested in different things at different times?

What really messed with my understanding was Coinbase on Friday. They missed earnings but the stock jumped 16% anyway. That seems completely backwards until you realize that for crypto stocks, Bitcoin's price probably matters more than the company's quarterly numbers. Which makes sense but also feels weird because I thought earnings were supposed to be the main thing that mattered. Guess different sectors play by different rules.

PayPal is bothering me. It's trading at a P/E of 7.22 which is insanely low compared to everything else I'm watching, but it just keeps dropping. I know low P/E can mean value OR it can mean nobody wants the stock for good reason. But how do you actually tell the difference? I think the answer is probably "read the earnings reports and understand the business" but I haven't done that yet. Need to actually dig in instead of just looking at numbers.

One pattern I'm noticing is that my watchlist is super concentrated in tech. Three of seven stocks are Apple, Microsoft, and Nvidia, and they all kind of move together. When AI hype is up, they're all up. When people get nervous, they all drop. That's probably bad for diversification but these are also the companies I actually understand and use. Not sure if I should force myself to add healthcare or energy stocks just for diversity when I don't really know those industries yet.

JPMorgan has been the most boring stock on my list and maybe that's actually the point. It barely moves, P/E is 15 instead of 33+, and it just kind of exists and makes money. Everyone on Twitter gets hyped about explosive tech plays but banks just sit there being stable. Starting to think boring might be good when you're trying to build something long-term.

Still have a ton of questions. Why do different sectors have totally different "normal" P/E ratios? When should you actually sell something that's dropping versus holding it? How much do I need to understand macro stuff like interest rates and unemployment versus just focusing on individual companies? Feels like I'm supposed to know all of this already but I'm figuring it out as I go.

Nvidia earnings are coming up on February 25th and that feels like it's going to be a big deal. If they crush it maybe the AI rotation reverses and tech comes back. If they disappoint it might confirm that people are right to be moving their money elsewhere. Either way I need to pay attention.

Overall this week taught me that markets are way more nuanced than "stocks go up or stocks go down." There's this whole layer of why things are moving and where money is going that I'm just starting to see. It's overwhelming but also kind of exciting because every day I understand a little bit more.

Next week I want to actually read PayPal's earnings report instead of just looking at ratios, maybe find one or two healthcare stocks to start learning about, and definitely watch what happens with Nvidia on the 25th. Also need to figure out if I should be tracking broader economic indicators or if that's too much right now.

One step at a time.
