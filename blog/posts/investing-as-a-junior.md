# Why I Started Investing at 17

I opened my first brokerage account in November of sophomore year. Not because I had some grand master plan or because my parents forced me — just because I got curious about where money actually goes when people say "I'm investing."

Most of my friends think investing is this complicated thing that only rich people do. And yeah, I thought that too for a while. But the more I looked into it, the more I realized it's actually... kind of just knowing where to put your money and then waiting. Which sounds boring until you realize waiting is basically the whole strategy.

## Why I Even Cared

I've been running a side project for a couple years now. Nothing huge, but it makes enough that I actually have money sitting in my bank account. And I started noticing this feeling: my money was just... sitting there. Doing nothing. Getting a pathetic interest rate (like 0.01% in a regular savings account, which is essentially nothing).

Then I listened to a podcast about compound interest and it clicked. The whole point is that if you start early, your money has *time* to grow. Ten years of 7% returns is wildly different than one year of 7% returns. And I have like 50+ years until retirement. That's actually a lot of time.

## The Confusing Part

Here's what threw me off initially: there's so much gatekeeping and jargon. People throw around terms like "diversification," "expense ratios," "index funds" like everyone's supposed to know what they mean. I didn't.

So I just... looked it up. Turns out most of it is simpler than the way people explain it. An index fund is literally just a collection of stocks. You buy a small piece of like 500 companies instead of trying to pick one winner. The expense ratio is just a tiny fee they charge you for managing it (usually less than 1%).

The biggest revelation: I don't actually need to be good at picking stocks. In fact, most people who try to pick stocks lose to just buying index funds over time. So I just... don't do that.

## What I Actually Did

I opened an account with a brokerage (won't name them, but there are like five good ones). Moved some money over. And then basically bought a total market index fund. That's it. Now that money is in like, thousands of companies, spread across different industries.

Is it thrilling? Not really. I check it maybe once a month. Sometimes it's up 2%, sometimes down 1%. It's boring, which is actually good — that means I'm not obsessing over it or making emotional decisions.

## The Real Thing I'm Learning

What's actually valuable isn't picking the perfect investment. It's just *starting*. It's getting comfortable with the idea that your money can work for you instead of against you. It's understanding that time is literally money when it comes to investing.

Plus, I'm learning way more than I would from a textbook. I'm reading annual reports. I'm looking at how different sectors perform. I'm thinking about where the economy is going. It's actually interesting when it's your money.

## What Comes Next

I'm thinking about learning more about specific companies, not to pick winners, but just to understand how businesses work. That seems actually useful for the whole finance + tech direction I'm thinking about for college.

Also I should probably mention: I'm not telling you to go open a brokerage account tomorrow. I'm just saying that if you're curious about how money works and you have money to put somewhere, there's no reason to wait until you're 30. You're allowed to start now.
